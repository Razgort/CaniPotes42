## Who Perrine Is

Perrine is one of ~10 administrators of a canicross club affiliated with the FFSLC. She's 38, a regular canitrail runner, and the person who actually makes things happen in the club — event creation, member communication, document management. She volunteers 5-8 hours a week on club admin on top of her day job. She didn't sign up to be an IT manager, but that's increasingly what the role demands.

Marie, 45, is Perrine's CNEAC counterpart — president of a small agility club (~60 members) who manages everything on paper. Marie has never had a digital tool for her club. She represents the greenfield adoption path. The persona below applies to both, with Marie-specific notes where they diverge.

---

## Psychological Profile

Perrine values **competence and control** in her domain. She knows her club inside-out — who runs which distances, which dogs need special attention, which members are reliable for volunteering. In the club context, she's confident and decisive. But when technology fails her — broken GPS placement, events that won't publish, chat that nobody checks — she feels **helpless and frustrated**. The tool is supposed to amplify her competence, not undermine it.

She has a **low tolerance for complexity** — not because she's incapable, but because she's time-poor. Every minute spent wrestling with a tool is a minute not spent on the sport she loves. She's tried CaniClub Connect (FFSLC path) and found it painful: GPS that doesn't place pins correctly, chat that nobody uses because it's unreliable, a dated interface that makes the club look amateur.

Marie (CNEAC path) has an even simpler relationship with tools: she has **none**. New member registrations happen on paper forms at training sessions. Vaccine certificates are photocopied and filed in a binder. License tracking is an Excel sheet. She's not anti-technology — she just never had a canine-sports tool offered to her. She's **open but cautious**: one bad experience could close the door for years.

Both Perrine and Marie share a core trait: they are **passionate volunteers, not paid employees**. Their motivation is love for the sport and community. Any tool that feels like work — configuration, training, maintenance — will be abandoned. The tool must feel like a **natural extension of what they already do**, not a new thing to learn.

---

## Internal State

When Perrine thinks about club management tools, she feels **resigned frustration**. She's accepted that CaniClub Connect is broken but has no alternative. She creates workarounds — sharing GPS coordinates via WhatsApp because the in-app maps don't work, posting events on Facebook because the club platform's chat is dead. Each workaround erodes her confidence that a better solution exists.

When Marie thinks about going digital, she feels **cautious hope mixed with fear**. She's seen other associations adopt tools and either love them or waste months on failed implementations. She doesn't want to be the president who forced a bad tool on 60 members. The stakes feel personal — her credibility is on the line.

Both feel **protective of their members' experience**. They won't adopt something that makes their members' lives harder, even if it makes admin easier. The members' experience IS the test.

---

## Usage Context

**Access/Discovery:** Perrine hears about CaniFed through another club admin at a FFSLC event or regional meeting. Peer recommendation is the trigger. Marie finds it through a canine sports Facebook group or association network. Both visit the website skeptically.

**Emotional State:** Cautiously interested but ready to bounce at the first friction. Past bad experiences (Perrine with CaniClub Connect, Marie with nothing working at all) create a "prove it" mindset. The first 10 minutes decide everything.

**Behavior Pattern:** Perrine scans for: does GPS work? Can I create an event quickly? Will my members actually use this? She tests by creating a sample event with a GPS pin. If that works smoothly, she's intrigued. If it's clunky, she's gone. Marie scans for: is this simple? Can I add my members? Where do documents go?

**Decision Criteria:** (1) Zero cost — no budget approval needed. (2) Works on first try — no training required. (3) Members will actually use it — the interface must be obvious. (4) No lock-in — she can leave if it doesn't work out.

**Success Outcome:** Club is set up in under 10 minutes. First event created with GPS pin in under 2 minutes. First members invited and active within the week. Perrine/Marie feels confident showing it to the rest of the admin team.

## Driving Forces

### Wants (Positive Drivers)

**✅ Set up the club in 10 minutes with zero training**
- WHAT: Instant, guided onboarding that creates a functional club space
- WHY: She's a volunteer with no time for configuration; one evening is all she'll give
- WHEN: First contact with the platform, the make-or-break moment
- **CaniFed Promise:** Guided club creation flow: name, logo, invite link — done. No settings pages, no documentation, no "contact support."

**✅ See all members, dogs, and documents in one dashboard**
- WHAT: Unified view of who's in the club, their dogs, uploaded documents, license status
- WHY: Currently scattered across Excel, paper, and federation platforms; she wastes time cross-referencing
- WHEN: Weekly admin check-ins, before training sessions, when preparing for competitions
- **CaniFed Promise:** One dashboard with member list, dog profiles, document status, and license tracking. Search, filter, export. Everything in one place.

**✅ Create events with GPS in two taps**
- WHAT: Smooth event creation with map-based GPS pin placement that actually works
- WHY: This is CaniClub Connect's most hated failure; broken GPS is the #1 complaint
- WHEN: Every time she plans a training session, rando, or competition — near-daily for active clubs
- **CaniFed Promise:** Drop a pin on the map, add time and description, publish. Members tap for Waze/Google Maps directions. It just works.

### Fears (Negative Drivers)

**❌ Migration disrupts the club mid-season**
- WHAT: Switching tools causes confusion, lost data, and member frustration during the active period
- WHY: The club's season is October-April (canicross) or year-round (agility); there's no "quiet time" to switch
- WHEN: The adoption decision moment — weighing the risk of change vs. the pain of status quo
- **CaniFed Answer:** Parallel running: keep the old tool while CaniFed proves itself. No data migration required — start fresh, import members gradually. No commitment, no contract.

**❌ The tool requires ongoing technical maintenance**
- WHAT: Software updates, bug fixes, configuration changes that demand tech skills she doesn't have
- WHY: She's a volunteer, not an IT admin; the previous referent who set up CaniClub Connect left and nobody knows how it works
- WHEN: After adoption, when the initial enthusiasm fades and the tool needs to sustain itself
- **CaniFed Answer:** Zero maintenance. Cloud-hosted, auto-updated, no server config, no plugin management. The club admin manages club content, not infrastructure.

**❌ Members won't adopt and will revert to Facebook/WhatsApp**
- WHAT: The new tool becomes a ghost town while real coordination stays on social media
- WHY: She's seen it before — the club signed up for CaniClub Connect's chat but everyone stayed on Facebook
- WHEN: First weeks after launch, the critical adoption window
- **CaniFed Answer:** Event notifications that pull members in. Working GPS that's immediately useful. Chat that replaces, not duplicates, existing groups. Value on first open.

## Transformation Journey

**Before CaniFed:**
Perrine spends hours on admin that should take minutes. GPS pins don't work, so she screenshots Google Maps and posts on WhatsApp. New member registrations are a multi-step paper-to-digital dance. She feels like an unpaid IT worker instead of a club organizer.

Marie manages everything in binders and Excel. Registration day is chaos. She can't easily verify who paid, who has a valid license, which dogs have current vaccinations. She knows there must be a better way but doesn't know where to start.

**After CaniFed:**
Perrine creates next week's training session in 90 seconds — pin, time, description, publish. Members get notified and tap for directions. She checks the dashboard to see who's registered, spots a new member whose vaccine cert is expiring, sends a quick reminder. She feels like the competent organizer she actually is.

Marie watches a new member register online, upload their dog's vaccine certificate, and appear in her dashboard — all without a paper form. She smiles. This is what she imagined digital could be.

## Relationship to Business Goals

- ⭐ **Club Adoption (THE ENGINE):** Perrine/Marie IS the decision-maker. One admin convinced = one club onboarded = 30-150 new users. Every acquisition starts here.
- 🚀 **Member Engagement:** Her event creation quality directly drives member engagement. If her events are easy to find, GPS works, and notifications land — members engage.
- 🌟 **Club Autonomy:** She benefits most from zero lock-in. If CaniFed works, she stays. If not, she leaves. This freedom paradoxically builds trust.
